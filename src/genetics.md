### Asexual vs. Sexual Reproduction

Now, we know that sexual reproduction is when 2 organisms release sex cells (typically sperm and egg), with each one containing **half** the amount of DNA/genetic material. This way, each one of your genes contains alleles from *both* parents, not just one. For example, if your father had blue eyes and your mother had green eyes, assuming no mutations occur, the sperm cell will contain **one** allele for the blue eye trait variation, and the egg cell will contain **one** allele for the green eye trait variation. Now, we know that it's only one allele per sex cell because your chromosomes are sorted into **pairs**. Humans have 46 chromosomes, so humans have 23 chromosome pairs (check what chromosomes are in vocab table). These alleles from both parents make up the genotype, and this genotype will then influence what proteins are created, and if the proteins should/shouldn't do their job. This all results in the phenotype we see.

Now for asexual reproduction. Asexual reproduction happens with only **one** organism, and is not possible for most animals (dogs, cattle, humans). Asexual reproduction usually happens via 4 methods: **fragmentation, budding, binary fission, and parthogenesis**.

**Fragmentation:**
Fragmentaton is when.a chunk of an asexual species (like coral) breaks off, and becomes its own chunk. It finds a suitable place to live and grow, and it'll then become its own organism.

**Budding:**
Budding is when an asexual organism clones itself and has the clone “bud” off of the parent organism. This is typically found among plants, such as the spider plant, which grows offshoots which then grow “daughter” plants, creating an offspring.

**Binary Fission:**
Binary fission is when a single-celled asexual organism (like bacteria) creates a copy of its genetic material and then splits into two cells.

**Parthogenesis:**
Lastly, parthogenesis is when a species of an asexual organism doesn’t require eggs to be fertilized, and so offspring can be created with just a female and no sperm being needed.

All these methods "clone" the original plant, and contain the **exact same genetic material as the parent**, unless a mutation occurs.
